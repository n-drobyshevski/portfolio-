Three ways to make the page an event

Each one is a working prototype: open it, scroll, click the controls, try it on your phone. They share the calm paper colours, the type from A, and the same words, so you are only comparing the idea and the motion.

  1. Prototype 1

    Print Run

    The page is a small riso press. Every scene is printed in front of you, ink pass by pass, then carried onto a pile of finished sheets. The tabbed pile is the menu, and the job ticket with "Get a quote" never leaves the screen.

    • Scroll to print the next sheet, or click a tab to run the press at speed.
    • On a phone, sheets stack vertically with a bottom bar for Menu, Prices and Get a quote.
    • "Animations off" on the job ticket shows everything as plain printed sheets.
    Open Print Run
  2. Prototype 2

    The Sentence

    The whole site is one hand-set line: "I make websites for small businesses." Each word is a link with a small label under it. On the first scroll the words lift out of the line and settle in the margin as the menu, and every word does its own trick when it opens its page.

    • "make" spreads into four steps, "websites" splits into web and sites around your work, "small" becomes "No small print." on the prices.
    • The full stop swells and floods the screen dark for the contact page. The "Prices" bookmark ribbon is always one click away.
    • On a phone, a Menu slip at the bottom carries a tiny copy of the sentence. The French version is still a placeholder here.
    Open The Sentence
  3. The Leaflet is still being built
    Prototype 3

    The Leaflet

    A folded printed leaflet lies on a desk and opens one crease at a time in 3D as you scroll. Fully open it lies flat like a poster, then turns over like real paper to show the reply slip on its back.
